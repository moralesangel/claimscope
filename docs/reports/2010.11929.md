## Tested claims

### vit_dominates_compute_performance_tradeoff

> Across the scaling study, Vision Transformers achieve the same transfer performance as ResNets using roughly 2-4x less pre-training compute.

Source: Fig. 5, Sec. 4.4 · Metric: transfer accuracy vs pre-training compute (exaFLOPs) · Expected: ViT compute-to-performance ratio < ResNet compute-to-performance ratio

**Verdict: Not consistent at reduced scale**

vit: 0.1761 (sd 0.0702, n=3); resnet_bit: 0.816 (sd 0.0738, n=3). Effect in the predicted direction: -0.64 [95% CI -0.7349, -0.5477], Welch p=0.00041. With 3 seeds per arm this test has low power: an inconclusive result means insufficient evidence, not absence of an effect.

#### Reduction plan

_This is what was **designed**. The experiment was then written by a model from this description, so read the script in the workspace to see what actually ran -- the two can differ, for instance where the sandbox has no network and the data had to be generated instead of downloaded._

**Original setup.** In the paper, ViT and ResNet (BiT) variants of multiple sizes are pre-trained on JFT-300M/ImageNet-21k at various compute budgets, then fine-tuned/transferred to downstream tasks; the paper plots transfer accuracy vs. pre-training compute (exaFLOPs) and shows ViT models reach the same accuracy as ResNets using roughly 2-4x less compute.

**Reduced setup.** Using sklearn's 8x8 digits dataset (1797 images, 10 classes) as a stand-in for the image-classification transfer task. Build two families of small numpy-implemented models at 3 sizes each (varying hidden width/number of blocks) to sweep a compute axis: (1) a 'ViT-like' arm: a patch-based self-attention model that splits each 8x8 image into 4 2x2 patches, embeds them, and applies 1-2 layers of self-attention + MLP (implemented by hand in numpy with manual forward/backward via simple gradient descent, or via a tiny closed-form logistic classifier trained with SGD on top of an attention-pooled representation) -- the attention mechanism is preserved as the defining architectural feature; (2) a 'ResNet-like' arm: a small convolution-analogue model built from strided/local weighted sums (implemented as a locally-connected linear layer stack with residual/skip additions in numpy), matched in FLOPs at each of 3 compute levels to the corresponding ViT model. Train each model with plain SGD for a fixed small number of epochs at 3 compute budgets (e.g., corresponding to ~1x, 2x, 4x FLOPs by varying epochs*width), record test accuracy on a held-out 20% split, and estimate FLOPs analytically from matrix-multiply counts. Repeat with 3 random seeds per arm per compute level (train/val split reshuffled and weights reinitialized). Compare accuracy vs. estimated-FLOPs curves for the two arms to see whether the ViT-like arm reaches equal accuracy at lower FLOPs than the ResNet-like arm.

**Why the claim should transfer.** The claim is about a *relative* efficiency ordering (ViT reaches a given accuracy at lower compute than ResNet), which is a comparative property that plausibly depends more on the qualitative difference between global self-attention and local/residual convolutional mixing than on absolute scale or dataset size. Digits (8x8, 10 classes, 1797 samples) is small enough that neither model family will trivially saturate accuracy at the smallest compute budget -- a locally-connected model with only a couple of small receptive-field layers and limited epochs will underfit somewhat relative to a global-attention model that can mix information across all 4 patches from the first layer, so there is room for a genuine gap to appear at low compute and a room for it to close as compute increases, mirroring the shape the paper reports. Using multiple compute levels (varying width/epochs) with matched analytic FLOPs keeps the sweep structure of Fig. 5 rather than reducing it to one point. If the ViT-like arm's global-mixing advantage is a real, mechanism-level property (as argued in the paper) rather than an artifact of JFT-scale pretraining, it should show up here as reaching a comparable accuracy at a smaller FLOPs budget than the ResNet-like arm, even though absolute accuracies and FLOPs are tiny compared to the paper. If no such gap appears, that is itself informative: it suggests the claimed efficiency advantage may depend on the large-scale pretraining regime rather than being a scale-free architectural property, which is a legitimate (if negative) answer to whether the direction transfers.

**Seeds per arm:** 3 · **Estimated cost:** 55 min · **Code source:** from_scratch

**Changes from the paper**

- Dataset changed from JFT-300M/ImageNet-21k (millions of large images) to sklearn's 8x8 digits (1797 tiny images) -- no network access is available, and digits is the only image-like classification set on hand; the claim is about a *relative* compute-accuracy ordering between two architecture families, a comparative and largely scale-free property of self-attention vs. convolutional/residual layers, so a much smaller image task can still expose whether one family is more compute-efficient than the other.
- Architectures reimplemented from scratch in plain numpy (no PyTorch/JAX/TF available) as minimal analogues: a patch+self-attention model (ViT-like) and a locally-connected/residual model (ResNet-like) -- functionally preserves the key structural difference the claim is about (global self-attention mixing vs. local convolutional/residual mixing) while fitting the numpy-only constraint.
- Model depth/width and pretraining corpus drastically shrunk (few hundred to few thousand parameters, 3 model sizes, training in tens of seconds each) instead of the paper's family of models spanning many exaFLOPs -- necessary to fit a 60-minute CPU-only budget; only the *relative ordering* of compute-vs-accuracy curves is being tested, not the absolute compute values, so shrinking magnitude should not by itself change which architecture is more efficient.
- FLOPs are estimated analytically (matrix-multiply counts) rather than measured with a hardware profiler -- adequate at this scale since both arms use the same counting convention, so any bias in the estimate is roughly consistent across arms and shouldn't flip the comparison.
- 3 compute budgets are created by varying epochs and hidden width rather than by training genuinely different published model sizes -- this preserves the idea of a compute sweep (multiple points on a compute axis per arm) needed to compare curves, while staying within budget.

**Deliberately preserved**

- The comparative nature of the claim: both arms are evaluated on the exact same dataset, same train/test split protocol, same number of seeds, and same compute-budget levels, so the only difference is architecture (attention-based mixing vs. local/residual mixing).
- The core architectural distinction: one arm uses global self-attention across patches (the defining feature of ViT), the other uses local/residual convolutional-style mixing (the defining feature of ResNet/BiT) -- not swapped for an unrelated method.
- A genuine compute-accuracy sweep with multiple budget levels per arm, matched in estimated FLOPs across arms at each level, so the comparison is a curve-vs-curve comparison as in the paper's Fig. 5, not a single-point comparison.
- At least 3 seeds per arm per compute level, to give a noise estimate around each accuracy value.

### hybrid_beats_vit_small_compute_only

> Hybrid models (CNN feature maps fed into a Transformer) slightly outperform plain ViT at small computational budgets, but this advantage disappears for larger models.

Source: Fig. 5, Sec. 4.4 · Metric: transfer accuracy vs pre-training compute · Expected: hybrid > ViT at small compute; hybrid ≈ ViT at large compute

**Verdict: Inconclusive**

This claim predicts the arms are alike ("hybrid > ViT at small compute; hybrid ≈ ViT at large compute"), not that one beats the other. Settling it needs an equivalence test against a stated margin, which this analysis does not implement. A one-sided comparison would read a difference as the claim holding up and no difference as inconclusive, inverting it. The runs are in the workspace.

#### Reduction plan

_This is what was **designed**. The experiment was then written by a model from this description, so read the script in the workspace to see what actually ran -- the two can differ, for instance where the sandbox has no network and the data had to be generated instead of downloaded._

**Original setup.** In the ViT paper (Fig. 5, Sec 4.4), hybrid ResNet+ViT models and plain ViT models are pre-trained on JFT-300M / ImageNet-21k at varying compute budgets, then transfer-accuracy is measured; hybrids outperform plain ViT at small pre-training compute but the gap vanishes at larger model/compute scales.

**Reduced setup.** Use sklearn's 8x8 digits dataset (1797 images, 10 classes) standing in for the ImageNet/JFT pre-training + transfer setup. Flatten each 8x8 image into 4 non-overlapping 4x4 patches (2x2 grid of patches, 4 tokens, 16 dims each) to build a minimal patch-token sequence, mimicking ViT's patchify step. Implement two from-scratch numpy models at matched small-compute budget: (1) "ViT": patches -> linear embedding (16->8 dims) -> 1-head self-attention block (with residual) -> mean pool -> linear classifier. (2) "Hybrid": a tiny fixed 3x3 convolution (single channel, mean/edge filter, non-trainable "CNN stem") applied to the 8x8 image first to produce a lightly-processed 8x8 map, which is then patchified and fed through the identical embedding+attention+classifier stack as ViT. Both models are trained with the same number of gradient steps (200 steps, batch size 32, plain SGD, lr=0.05) on a random 300-image training subset (small-data regime to induce a visible train/test gap), evaluated on a held-out 500-image test subset. To create a genuine 'small compute' vs 'larger compute' comparison, run each arch at two capacity points: (a) small: embedding dim 8, 1 attention head, 200 steps ("small compute"); (b) large: embedding dim 32, 2 attention heads, 800 steps ("large compute"). Compare hybrid vs ViT accuracy gap at each compute point, expecting gap>0 at small compute and gap≈0 at large compute.

**Why the claim should transfer.** The paper's mechanism is that a local-feature-extracting front end (CNN) gives the model useful inductive bias when data/compute is too limited for pure self-attention to learn good spatial structure from scratch, and this advantage is washed out once the plain attention model has enough compute/data to learn equivalent representations itself. This is a general small-data/small-compute phenomenon, not one specific to ImageNet scale: with only 300 training images and a single attention head at 200 steps, plain ViT should still be in a regime where it hasn't learned good token relations, so a hard-coded local-averaging prior (the fixed conv stem) should give an edge. At the larger compute point (800 steps, more heads/dims), both architectures have enough optimization budget to reach similar train/test performance, and the fixed conv prior becomes less differentiating (or possibly even slightly limiting since it's non-learned), which is expected to shrink the gap -- matching the claimed direction. The visible train/test accuracy gap expected in the baseline (300 training examples vs 500 test, small models) confirms we're in the underfit-but-data-scarce regime where inductive bias should matter, not a regime where both models already saturate performance.

**Seeds per arm:** 3 · **Estimated cost:** 45 min · **Code source:** from_scratch

**Changes from the paper**

- JFT-300M/ImageNet-21k pre-training + downstream transfer replaced by direct train/test split on load_digits -- the claim is about relative architecture ordering under varying compute, which is a property of the attention/conv inductive-bias interaction, not the specific dataset scale; using a small labeled dataset directly (train acc vs test acc) is a fair proxy for 'transfer accuracy' at this budget.
- Patch grid reduced from 16x16 (ViT-Base) to 2x2 (4 patches of 4x4 pixels) on 8x8 images -- preserves the core patchify+attention mechanism while fitting an 8x8 image; the relative comparison (hybrid vs plain) only needs *some* patch structure, not a specific resolution.
- CNN stem replaced by a single fixed (non-learned) 3x3 convolution filter instead of a full trainable ResNet stem -- keeps the 'local feature aggregation before attention' inductive bias that defines 'hybrid' while remaining cheap and CPU-tractable; since both arms otherwise share identical downstream layers, the *architectural difference under test* (local conv prior vs none) is preserved.
- Framework reimplemented in raw numpy (forward+backward by hand) since no PyTorch/TensorFlow/JAX available -- mathematically identical operations (attention, embedding, linear classifier) at tiny scale; this is an implementation-language change, not a methodological one.
- Compute budget reduced from millions of examples / GPU-days to two fixed step counts (200 vs 800) run entirely on CPU with tiny models -- 'small compute' and 'large compute' are relative within this experiment, matching the claim's structure (gap at low compute, no gap at high compute) rather than absolute FLOP values from the paper.
- Training set shrunk to 300 images (rather than the full 1797) -- deliberately pushes into a small-data / limited-capacity regime so a visible train/test gap exists, giving room for an inductive-bias advantage (hybrid's local conv prior) to manifest, mirroring why hybrids help ViT when data/compute is scarce.

**Deliberately preserved**

- The two architectures under comparison remain distinguished only by the presence/absence of a local-feature (conv-like) preprocessing step before an identical attention+embedding+classifier pipeline -- exactly the axis the claim is about.
- The 'small compute vs large compute' structure of the claim: two matched capacity/step settings per architecture, so the direction (gap shrinks with more compute) can be observed within one run.
- Same dataset, same train/test split, same number of steps, same optimizer and learning rate for both arms at each compute point -- only the model differs (invariant 1).
- Same number of seeds (3) per arm per compute point, with different random inits/seeds for weight initialization and data shuffling.

## Claims not tested

- **`vit_l16_beats_bitl_jft`** (comparative) — ViT-L/16 pre-trained on JFT-300M outperforms BiT-L (ResNet152x4, also pre-trained on JFT-300M) on all evaluated downstream tasks while using substantially less pre-training compute. _Both arms require pre-training on JFT-300M scale data with huge models, far beyond a 60-minute CPU-only budget._
- **`vit_h14_sota_accuracy`** (absolute) — The largest model, ViT-H/14, reaches 88.55% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, 90.72% on ImageNet-ReaL, 94.55% on CIFAR-100, and 77.63% on the 19-task VTAB suite. _This is an absolute headline-number claim tied to a specific huge model and dataset, not testable by construction._
- **`vit_h14_less_compute_than_noisy_student`** (comparative) — ViT-H/14 achieves accuracy on par with or better than the state-of-the-art Noisy Student and BiT-L models while requiring substantially fewer TPUv3-core-days to pre-train. _Requires reproducing state-of-the-art large pretrained models (ViT-H/14, Noisy Student, BiT-L) whose compute and scale cannot fit a CPU-only budget._
- **`vit_h14_beats_bit_on_vtab_natural_structured`** (comparative) — On the VTAB benchmark, ViT-H/14 outperforms BiT-R152x4 and other prior methods on the Natural and Structured task groups, while being roughly comparable on the Specialized group. _Depends on massive pretrained ViT-H/14 and BiT-R152x4 models on JFT-300M, which cannot be reproduced at small scale._
- **`vit_underperforms_on_small_data_scale_helps`** (scaling_trend) — When pre-trained on smaller datasets like ImageNet without strong regularization, ViT models perform worse than comparable ResNets, but this gap closes and reverses as pre-training dataset size increases to ImageNet-21k and JFT-300M. _The claim's effect is explicitly attributed to pre-training dataset size (ImageNet vs ImageNet-21k vs JFT-300M), making it scale-dependent and undermined by shrinking._
- **`larger_vit_benefits_more_from_larger_pretraining_data`** (scaling_trend) — Larger ViT variants only overtake smaller ViT variants in performance once pre-trained on sufficiently large datasets (e.g., JFT-300M), whereas on smaller pre-training data smaller models are comparable or better. _This is an explicit scaling-law claim about how effects depend on pre-training data size, which shrinking would destroy._
- **`vit_overfits_more_on_small_jft_subsets`** (comparative) — On small subsets of JFT-300M, Vision Transformers overfit more than ResNets of comparable compute, performing worse on very small subsets but better once the subset size is large enough (e.g., 90M+ images). _The claim's core point depends on comparing very small vs very large (90M+) JFT subsets, and requires JFT-scale data unavailable on a CPU-only budget._
- **`self_supervised_vit_gain_vs_scratch`** (absolute) — Using a masked-patch-prediction self-supervised pre-training objective, ViT-B/16 reaches 79.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, a 2 percentage point improvement over training from scratch, but still about 4 points below the equivalent supervised pre-training result. _This is an absolute-number claim (79.9% top-1, 2pt and 4pt gaps) tied to specific pre-training regimes, not reproducible at reduced scale by construction._

## How these experiments were run

> **These results were produced without container isolation.** The experiments ran in a restricted subprocess, with network access blocked and resources capped, but not inside a sandbox that can contain what it runs. Treat the results as a demonstration of the pipeline rather than as a trustworthy measurement, and re-run under Docker before relying on them.

## Limitations

Read these results narrowly.

- **A negative result here does not refute the paper.** These experiments run at a fraction of the
  original scale. An effect that disappears when the model, the data, or the training budget shrinks
  may still be real at full scale; that is precisely what reduced-scale testing cannot tell us.
- **A positive result is not a reproduction.** It says the direction of the claim survived
  shrinking, not that the paper's numbers were reproduced.
- **Seed counts are small.** With a handful of seeds per arm, statistical power is low. An
  inconclusive verdict usually means there was not enough evidence either way, not that the effect
  is absent.
- **Absolute claims are never testable this way** and are recorded without being run.
- The reduced experiments were written from the paper's description, so they may differ from the
  authors' implementation in ways that matter.
